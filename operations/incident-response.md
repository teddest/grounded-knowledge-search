# What counts as an incident

An incident includes suspected unauthorized access, exposure of customer data, a material service outage, or a failure that affects multiple customer workspaces. A single routine support question is not an incident unless it indicates a broader pattern.

# First response

The person who discovers a possible incident opens an incident record, records the discovery time, preserves relevant evidence, and alerts the on-call Security and Reliability lead. Do not delete logs, restart systems solely to hide symptoms, or share incident details in public channels.

# Customer data exposure

For suspected customer-data exposure, restrict access to the affected material when safe, identify the potentially affected workspaces, and notify Security and Privacy immediately. Support should not independently determine whether notification is legally required.

# Communications

The incident lead owns internal status updates. Security and Privacy approves communications about privacy or regulatory impact. Customer Success coordinates customer updates using approved language. Employees should not speculate about root cause or scope.

# Closure

An incident closes after containment, recovery, evidence review, and a documented follow-up plan. The owner records timeline, impact, root cause when known, and corrective actions. Customer-facing commitments belong in the support or customer-success system of record.
